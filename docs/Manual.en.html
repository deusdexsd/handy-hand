Handy Hand

A notch-docked panel for macOS: your library of sounds, video and images, from which you drag files straight into your editing app.  ·  Polski

1. Installation and first steps

  1. Open handy-hand.dmg and drag the app to Applications.
  2. The app is signed ad-hoc (no Apple developer account). On first launch on another Mac: System Settings → Privacy & Security → Open Anyway, or in Terminal xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine "/Applications/Handy Hand.app".
  3. The panel opens when you move the cursor to the notch (or your chosen screen edge). The menu bar has the app icon: left click = Settings (click again or press Esc to close), right click = menu (panel mode, show/hide, setup guide, quit).
  4. Add sources: Add → folder / files, or just drag them from Finder onto the panel.
  5. Drag a tile onto your editor's timeline — files stay on disk, the app never modifies them.

2. Setup guide and tour

On first launch a seven-step guide appears. Step one is the language (Polski / English) — it instantly switches the whole guide, the tour and Settings. Then: where the panel lives, where files come from, how to show them, look, and a summary. Everything is saved immediately and you can skip it with “Skip — I'll set up later”. After the last step the panel dims and highlights each element with a short description (“what is what”) — Enter or a click goes on, Esc ends it.

Both are always available: right-click the menu bar icon (Setup guide…, Show what is what) or Settings → General → Help.

3. Views, size, layout

One toolbar icon cycles three views: list → grid → minimalist.

  • The size slider (strip below the list) works in every view; it is also in Settings.
  • Grid: thumbnails are square (1:1) by default; Settings → Appearance also offers 4:5 (portrait) and 5:4 (landscape).
  • Minimalist: equal-width columns, every tile in the media's own proportions, rounded corners, no names. Small icons and PNGs are not stretched. A checkbox in Settings hides names on sounds (on by default).
  • Toolbar order: hold ⌘ and drag icons (like the macOS menu bar). The sidebar icon stays on the left.
  • Hide the category sidebar with the icon at the left of the toolbar.

4. Organizing

  • Favorites (star, ⌘5), tags, sound types (SFX/Music), length ranges and keywords — in the context menu and Settings.
  • Collections (e.g. one per project): the same file can be in many collections at once without affecting favorites. Drag files onto a collection in the sidebar.
  • Selecting: click, ⇧-click (range), ⌘-click (single), ⌘A (all). Right click opens the file menu (favorite, collection, tag, rename, reveal in Finder, copy files).
  • Duplicates are collapsed by default (×N badge); turn it off in Settings → Sources.
  • Undo: ⌘Z / ⇧⌘Z undoes up to 100 organization changes (favorites, tags, collections, types, notes). It does not undo a file rename on disk or adding/removing sources.

5. Notes and tasks

  • The list icon in the toolbar opens the panel on the right.
  • A note is global or pinned to a collection, folder, subfolder or type. A note added while you are inside a collection/folder belongs to it right away.
  • Assign it with the folder-plus icon (menu with Type / Folders / Collections submenus) or drag the whole note onto an item in the left sidebar. Dropping it on “All” makes it global again.
  • The small caption under the text shows where the note lives (click to jump there). The Here / All switch shows the current view's notes or every note.
  • Edit: double-click the text. Delete: ×.

7. Settings

Open with a left click on the menu bar icon (click again or Esc to close) or the gear in the panel. Tabs: General (language PL/EN, help, login startup, panel behavior, autoplay, stop on collapse, placement), Sources, Appearance (accent, notch effect, waveform, item size, panel transparency, thumbnail ratio and quality, menu bar icon: Pointing hand, Open hand, HA HA, Waveform, Film frames), Lengths, Keywords, Shortcuts, Export.

  • Panel transparency: a slider from solid to clear; the middle is the default “glass”. The system “Reduce transparency” setting is respected too.
  • Thumbnail quality: low / normal / high (160 / 260 / 420 px). Lower uses less memory and scrolls faster on very large libraries.

8. Performance, data, limits

  • Indexing is incremental (only changed files, live via FSEvents). Thumbnails are loaded only for visible tiles and the cache is capped.
  • User data: ~/Library/Application Support/MidniteDock (folder name unchanged after the product rename so nothing is lost).
  • Everything runs locally — no internet, no accounts.
  • Not verified: running on a Mac other than the development one, and on very large libraries (hundreds of thousands of files) — the design targets it, but tests used a small set.
